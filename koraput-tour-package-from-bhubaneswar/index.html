Koraput tour from Bhubaneswar

Travel overnight and wake up in the hills. Bhubaneswar to Koraput is a night on the train or a long day on the road, and the Traveller meets you the moment you arrive.

Overnight trainabout 13 hours
By roadabout 500 km, 10–12 h
Days to keep2 to 3, plus travel
PickupKoraput station or hotel

Getting to Koraput

  • Train: the Hirakhand Express runs overnight from Bhubaneswar to Koraput and back. Book sleeper or 3AC early for long weekends. Check the current timetable before booking.
  • Road: around 500 km via Berhampur and Rayagada. Ten to twelve hours with stops. Fine for a group leaving early; tiring as a same-day return.
  • Air: fly to Visakhapatnam and continue by road (five to six hours via Araku) or by the Araku line train. Small regional flights to Jeypore run when scheduled; check before you rely on them.

A 3-day plan that fits the train

  1. Day 1
    Arrive, settle, an easy first day

    The driver meets your morning train. Breakfast, Sabara Srikhetra, the Tribal Museum, then Upper Kolab for the late afternoon light.

    Sabara SrikhetraTribal MuseumUpper Kolab Reservoir
  2. Day 2
    The highland circuit

    Deomali early, with time on top. Back through Nandapur's old capital to Rani Duduma before the light goes.

    DeomaliNandapurRani Duduma
  3. Day 3
    Duduma, then the evening train

    A long drive south-west to Duduma Waterfall and the Machkund valley, lunch on the way, and back to Koraput for the evening departure.

    Duduma WaterfallOnukadelli
Keep a buffer before your return train. Ghat roads and a good waterfall both take longer than the map suggests.

What the Traveller changes

Groups from Bhubaneswar usually arrive together and want to stay together. One 17-seater means one pickup, one route, one set of timings and nobody waiting for the second car. The driver knows the roads; the support team is on WhatsApp for changes.

What to carry

  • A jacket, even in October. Deomali is windy and cold at dawn.
  • Cash for small markets and entry tickets.
  • Water and snacks for the Duduma day; food stops are far apart.
  • Offline maps. Coverage drops in the valleys.
